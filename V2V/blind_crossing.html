How the message actually moves

Four steps, well under a second

01

Broadcast

Every equipped vehicle transmits a Basic Safety Message about ten times a second: position, heading, speed, brake state. Direct sidelink — no cell tower, no subscription.

02

Receive & Localize

Nearby vehicles pick up the broadcast directly and match it against their own GNSS fix — so they know exactly where the other vehicle is, with zero visibility.

03

Predict

Both vehicles continuously project their trajectories forward. If the paths intersect inside a dangerous time window, a conflict is flagged in milliseconds.

04

Warn & Act

The driver gets an alert immediately. In an ADAS-linked vehicle, the same signal can trigger automatic braking before a human ever could.

This is a simplified illustration of the message exchange proposed under India's draft AIS-230 standard, built for the "blind intersection" scenario — it is not a certified reference implementation. Real deployments layer this on top of, not instead of, radar, camera, and driver attention. Timings and thresholds here are chosen for clarity, not measured from hardware.
