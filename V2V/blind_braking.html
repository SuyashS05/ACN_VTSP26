How the warning actually spreads

Four steps, propagating down the queue

01

Detect & Brake

Vehicle 1's driver (or sensors) sees the hazard and brakes. This part is identical with or without V2V — the technology doesn't prevent the first incident.

02

Broadcast Instantly

The moment the brake is applied, a hard-brake event goes out over the sidelink — no waiting for the car behind to notice brake lights.

03

Propagate Down the Queue

Every following vehicle relays the same alert, so a car several vehicles back gets nearly the same near-zero-latency warning as the one right behind the hazard.

04

React Early

Extra seconds mean real options: brake gently and stop with room to spare, or move over and keep moving — instead of a last-second panic stop.

This is a simplified illustration of the "sudden braking ahead" scenario described under India's draft AIS-230 standard. V2V does not prevent the initial hazard — an animal, debris, a stopped vehicle — from occurring; it only shortens how long the vehicles behind take to find out about it. Timings and thresholds here are chosen for clarity, not measured from hardware.
